American Express in India: winning premium customers in a UPI-first market

Everyday payments in India now run on UPI, a rail global card networks can't use for credit. What should a premium, closed-loop card company do?

My answer: win the top of the market on service and travel value, and partner for the QR moment instead of fighting it.

The structural problem

  • Amex runs a closed loop. It issues the card, runs the network and signs up merchants. That gives it rich data and control of the experience, but it must build acceptance itself.
  • The everyday payment moved to the QR code. Small, frequent spends happen on UPI, where only RuPay credit cards can run.
  • Regulation shapes the field. Data localisation cost Amex 15 months of new customers. UPI exclusivity for RuPay keeps global networks out of the fastest-growing payment habit.

Options

OptionUpsideDownside
Double down on premium travel and lifestylePlays to Amex's brand and high spend per cardSmall market; heavy reward cost
Co-brand with an Indian bank to issue a RuPay companion card for UPIGives Amex customers rewards on daily QR spendsShares economics and the customer relationship
Push merchant acceptance in premium categories (travel, dining, luxury retail)Fixes the "is Amex accepted here?" doubtSlow and costly in a QR-first merchant base
Lead on servicing and dispute experienceTurns compliance into a selling point, after the 2025 penaltyHard to market; benefits show slowly

My recommendation

Combine the first two options: keep the premium core, and add a RuPay companion through a bank partner so cardholders earn points on daily QR spends. This is the same "credit on QR" product in my UPI × Credit project. Back it with the servicing play: automatic credit-balance refunds and a visible dispute tracker, the fix behind my dispute tracker prototype.
