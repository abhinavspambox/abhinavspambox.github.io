CRED: can a rewards app for good borrowers become a profitable lender?

CRED rewards people for paying credit card bills on time, then sells them more financial products. Its FY25 results show the model starting to pay for itself.

My answer: yes, if it protects trust in its credit decisions above everything else, because lending now pays for the rest of the product.

How the business works

  1. Acquire high-credit-score cardholders with rewards for paying card bills through CRED. The bill payment is free to the user.
  2. Engage them with more products on the same trusted base: UPI payments, a shopping store, vehicle and rent payments, investments.
  3. Monetise mainly through lending to the same members. Managed lending assets reached ₹22,000 crore in FY25, and gross margin was about 70%.

The proof that step 2 works: about 45% of active members used three or more products in FY25, and average revenue per user was around ₹2,000.

Product lessons

  • The free product is the acquisition channel. Bill payment earns little, but it filters for creditworthy users, which makes lending cheaper and safer.
  • Breadth drives revenue per user. Revenue grew 16% in FY25 while operating loss halved, a sign that more products per member raised revenue faster than cost.
  • A walled garden is a moat and a ceiling. Selecting only high-score users keeps credit risk low but caps the market size.

Risks

  • Regulation: credit card bill payments, payment aggregation and lending partnerships are all RBI-regulated. A rule change can hit the core loop.
  • Competition for frequency: Inc42 reports WhatsApp passed CRED in UPI market share and volume in June 2026.
  • Leadership change: Inc42 also reports founder Kunal Shah stepped away in 2026 to lead WhatsApp globally, with Miten Sampat as interim CEO.

What I would build next as a CRED PM

A credit health coach: a monthly view of each member's utilisation, upcoming dues and credit score drivers, with one action to take. It deepens trust (the asset lending depends on), gives a reason to open the app between bills, and creates better-informed borrowers.
